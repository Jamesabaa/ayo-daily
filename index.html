How to play Ayo Daily

Ayo has regional variations. This game uses the Oware/Abapa-style, single-lap rules, rather than relay-sowing Ayoayo rules.

  1. Your pits are on the bottom. A classic game starts with four seeds in each of the twelve pits. You move first.
  2. Sow counterclockwise. Choose one of your nonempty pits. All its seeds go one at a time into the following pits: right along your row, then left along the computer's row. Skip the starting pit whenever you pass it again. Never sow into the score stores.
  3. Capture two or three. If your last seed lands in an opponent's pit and makes its total exactly two or three, capture those seeds. Continue backwards through consecutive opponent pits containing two or three. Stop at any other count or at your own side.
  4. Leave your opponent something. If that capture would remove every seed on their side, the entire capture is cancelled. The sowing still counts as your turn.
  5. Feed an empty side. When the opponent has no seeds, you must choose a move that gives them seeds, if possible. Only legal moves have gold edges. If no legal move is available, the game ends and each player collects the seeds remaining on their own side.
  6. Win by collecting more seeds. Capturing 25 wins immediately. A score of 24 each is a draw. Turns alternate; there are no bonus turns.

Loop safeguard: this digital version also ends after the same board, scores and turn occur three times, or after 100 consecutive turns without a capture. Each player then collects the seeds on their own side.

Daily Challenge: a date-seeded sequence of legal moves creates a shared starting position, including any captured seeds. You play next. Everyone using this file gets the same position for the same UTC date. Replaying keeps that day's position; it is a daily match, not a guaranteed winning puzzle.
